# Portable RAW pipeline

The CPU reference path is intentionally scene-referred and linear until the display transform:

1. Validate runtime width, height, stride, bit depth, white/black levels and one of the four Bayer patterns.
2. Unpack the `uint16` sensor samples and subtract black level without gamma or sRGB conversion.
3. Replace only isolated CFA outliers when same-colour neighbours agree; leave structured bright detail intact.
4. Apply the profile's bounded radial flat-field polynomial and pack the frame into a normalized 16-bit plane, one sample per sensel, at two bytes per pixel. The plane holds the sensor's own signal: white balance is *not* folded in here, because its gains are mostly above one and clamping them into a plane that ends at the sensor's white level would clip the top stop of the red channel and make a half-scale red sensel indistinguishable from a blown one.
5. Choose the primary frame: the sharpest frame, discounted by the share of its sensels that reached the sensor's white level, because a frame whose highlights are blown is the frame with the least highlight data to merge.
6. Build a green-derived luma proxy per frame and remove its two-pixel CFA parity with a separable 1-2-1. Alignment then runs as a pyramid: a half-resolution search wide enough to cover the drift of a long burst, a full-resolution search that only corrects what the coarse pass found, and a parabolic fit on the error surface for the sub-pixel remainder. The candidate is first rescaled to the reference by the least-squares gain through the origin, which is the exposure ratio: without it the match cost is dominated by the difference between two exposures rather than by misalignment, and every frame of a re-metered or bracketed burst lands on an arbitrary shift.
7. Estimate motion from post-alignment disagreement. Moving samples remain available but receive a low temporal weight.
8. Merge. The burst is anchored to the brightest exposure it recorded - the exposure the camera metered - and every frame is placed in that exposure from the ISO and exposure time it actually used. The anchor is taken over the whole burst, not over the frames the alignment kept, so a rejected frame cannot move the exposure of the frames it does not touch. Samples are weighted by an ISO-interpolated read/shot-noise variance and refined by two robust Huber passes. Saturation is tested on each frame's own reading before that rescale, and a clipped sample is dropped whenever an unsaturated observation of the same sensel exists: that is what lets a frame the burst underexposed carry a highlight the metered exposure lost.
9. Measure the recovered range and carry it. A merged sample above one is highlight range that only exists because the burst held two exposures, and it is reported as `recoveredHighlightFraction`. The merge does not fold that range down here: how far above white a highlight really is only becomes clear once the white balance and the camera matrix have scaled the channels, so the range is left standing and mapped back inside the display later. A burst of one exposure never exceeds one, reports none, and nothing below touches its render.
10. Apply the burst white balance once, to the merged plane.
11. Multi-frame detail recovery. Every frame but the primary was resampled with interpolation on its own colour lattice, which attenuates the top of the band the merge exists to preserve. Its thresholds are measured on the primary, so the primary's variance is carried into the anchor exposure with it: a frame the merge brightened brings its read noise up as well, and a threshold that ignored that would measure the amplified grain as detail. The primary is the one observation never resampled, so its same-phase high-pass measures how much of a coefficient the merge lost; both sides are soft-thresholded against their own noise first, and the deficit that survives is added back, gated where the merge already found structure and reduced across the strongest transitions so it cannot ring against them.
12. Demosaic using directional green and chroma interpolation with edge-aware fallback.
13. Apply the configurable camera matrix. Then, if step 9 found range above white, map the plane's own white point back to just below white with an extended Reinhard shoulder. Every stage below this one clamps a channel at one, so a recovered highlight that is left above white is turned straight back into flat white and the bracket's extra range is thrown away; the shoulder is what keeps it, and it is applied before the denoise stages because those measure their thresholds against the level the frame is finally rendered at. The white point is a high percentile of the brightest channel, and a burst that recovered nothing is left at its metered exposure.
14. Edge-aware luma bilateral whose range scale is the post-merge noise model divided by the accepted frame count, then mild chroma-only spatial suppression. The luma pass is what removes the grain the merge leaves behind; its scale shrinks as frames grow, so a 32-frame burst is not over-smoothed.
15. Split luminance into a fine and a coarse base and compress the base toward the scene mean while boosting the detail layer, then apply the global tone curve, the highlight shoulder and halo-limited fine detail. This is the step that produces the "more range than one exposure" look; it is a local tone map, not exposure fusion, so the highlight range it works with is the range step 9 recovered and step 13 put back inside the display.
16. Emit a linear floating-point RGB result to the API; PPM export applies only the final sRGB encoding.

Between steps 11 and 15 the values are still scene-referred, so white balance can leave the brightest colour channel above one. Only the lower bound is clamped there; step 13 and the local tone map are the two places that decide what reaches white.

`GCAMRAW1` is a transfer/debug format, not a final image format. It never stores JPEG-derived pixels, demosaiced samples, or tone-mapped data.

## Error policy

Malformed frames, incompatible dimensions/Bayer patterns, invalid profiles, and an empty accepted set are hard errors. The CLI and C API report them; they do not return an unrelated fallback image.
